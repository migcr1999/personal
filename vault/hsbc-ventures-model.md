# HSBC Ventures: How Deals Happen

Links: [[index]], [[persona]], [[specialism-decision]], [[asia-venture-landscape]], [[coach-learnings]].

## Operating model (from the user, 2026-10-24)
- **Strategic investment team**, not a financial VC. Every investment needs a business partnership.
- **A strategic fit is required:** the startup must work with an HSBC business, e.g. a vendor whose tech HSBC uses.
- **A POC usually comes first**, to prove the tech works for the business before any investment. **The business team runs it, not Ventures:** e.g. for a commercial banking product, Commercial Banking's IT team works directly with the startup.
- **Then sponsorship from a business head (MD level)** is needed to make the investment.
- **So you can't source a company just because it looks good.** Sourcing means finding startups that solve a real HSBC business problem.

## Deal path
`Business problem → startup that solves it → POC with a business unit → business-head (MD) sponsor → investment`

## Implications for me
- **I can't run POCs.** The business IT teams own them. My value is upstream (spotting the business need and the startup) and in relationships with the business and IT teams who run POCs.
- **Business-head relationships are the real currency.** MD-level sponsors decide deals, and they're also a second-sponsor hedge (see [[specialism-decision]]).
- **Good sourcing starts from business pain points** in HSBC units, not from startup lists.

## Internal networking rule (from the user, 2026-10-24)
- Reaching out directly to managers and directors in other HSBC business units is fine.
- MD level is difficult. Go through a Ventures manager or director.
