# Jobs at AI Maturity

Links: [[index]], [[ai-vision]], [[future-of-work]], [[core-rules]], [[specialism-decision]].

*Long-range view. Most sources are theory or models, not data. Confidence: medium on categories 1-3, low-medium on the rest. Sits under [[ai-vision]].*

## The key idea
At full maturity, AI can do almost any **task**. What remains are **roles defined by something AI can't hold**: legal responsibility, ownership, human trust, physical presence, or scarce context. Skills stop being the moat; *position* becomes the moat.

## Seven durable categories
| # | Category | Why it survives | Examples | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accountability holders** | Law and society need a named human who carries liability | Licensed signatories, fiduciaries, board members, investment-committee voters, compliance officers | Medium |
| 2 | **Owners of capital and businesses** | Returns flow to owners. Someone decides what AI is pointed at | Founders, owner-operators, capital allocators | Medium-high |
| 3 | **Trust and relationship roles** | In high-stakes deals people prefer a human counterpart (trust bias research, mixed) | Deal-makers, negotiators, senior client advisers, leaders | Medium |
| 4 | **Judgment without objective outcomes** | Hard to train AI where success has no clear measure (Acemoglu) | Strategy, taste, novel bets, setting goals for AI | Medium |
| 5 | **Physical, unstructured work** | Robotics lags software | Skilled trades, field service, care | Medium (shrinks as robotics matures) |
| 6 | **Holders of scarce context** | Proprietary data, networks and access that AI can't reach | People who own networks, relationships, proprietary deal flow | Low-medium |
| 7 | **"Not worth automating" work** | Compute goes to bottlenecks; low-value work stays human (Restrepo) | Local, low-margin services | Low; low pay too |

## Evidence notes
- Full-automation models predict labour income collapses without redistribution (preprint models, not forecasts).
- Acemoglu: tasks with context-dependence and no objective outcome measure resist automation.
- Restrepo (Yale): much work persists because it isn't essential to growth, not because AI can't do it.
- Present data: only ~23% of US establishments have AI in regular workflows (Fortune, Oct 2026). Goldman estimates ~1 in 11 workers displaced, likely arriving with the next recession.
- Usage data shows a mix of augmentation and automation, with automation rising (Anthropic Economic Index; 2026 figures from secondary sources).

## Implications for me
- **The strongest durable position stacks categories 1 + 2 + 3 + 6:** an accountable decision-maker who allocates capital, is trusted in relationships, and owns scarce context or networks. That describes an **investment decision-maker or a business owner**, which matches [[ai-vision]].
- **Skill-only positions (any category-4 role without 1-3) are fragile.** That includes AI product skills by themselves.
- **Differentiation must come from position, not process.** Proprietary relationships, a specific domain network, and accountable decisions. Team-standard processes don't count ([[coach-learnings]]).
- **Category 5 and 7 are not targets.**
