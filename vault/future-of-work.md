# Future of Work: AI Displacement Landscape

Links: [[index]], [[ai-vision]], [[core-rules]], [[career-direction]], [[founder-mindset]].

*Evidence that informs strategy. It sits under [[ai-vision]], which stays the governing premise. Confidence: high = multiple primary sources; medium = one primary study; low = commentary or vendor claims.*

## 1. What is happening now (Oct 2026)
| Signal | Evidence | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Entry level gets squeezed first | Employment of software developers aged 22-25 down ~20% from 2024 peak (Stanford AI Index 2026). Job-finding rate for 22-25s in AI-exposed jobs down ~14% (Anthropic study). | Medium-high |
| Seniors gain, juniors lose | Senior postings +14.7%, entry-level -7.5% in the year to May 2026 (Indeed Hiring Lab). AI-adopting firms grew senior roles 32% vs junior 6% (Revelio Labs). | Medium |
| Generalist tech oversupplied, specialists short | Applicant surplus for generalist tech roles; shortages in AI/ML, cloud architecture and security (SG recruiter data). HK software development demand fell sharply in 2024 (Hays). | Low-medium |
| Layoffs are relabelled | AI is cited in ~21% of announced US job cuts (Challenger), yet companies rarely name automation formally. Shopee cut ~8% of global developers in Jun 2026, tied to AI. | Medium |
| Asia governments are warning | Singapore's president warned in 2026 that AI is weakening demand for entry-level graduate jobs. | Medium |
| Aggregate jobs not collapsed yet | US white-collar jobs grew ~3M since 2022. Displacement shows up first as hiring freezes, not mass firing. | High |

## 2. Where it's heading
- **Displacement by task, then by headcount.** The first effect is "fewer people each doing more", then fewer roles. Back-office, clerical, junior analysis and routine coding go first.
- **Declining roles (WEF Future of Jobs 2025, to 2030):** clerks, data entry, bank tellers, admin assistants. First appearance of graphic designers and legal secretaries in the top 10, a sign that AI now does knowledge work.
- **Growing roles (WEF):** big data, fintech, AI/ML, security. Read these as near-term: under [[ai-vision]], technical specialist roles also fold into the business over time.
- **Finance:** junior IB analysts are the most exposed role in banking. Banks plan back-office cuts (one bank: 7,000+ by 2030). Relationship, negotiation and deal-judgment work stays human-led. Analysts move from building models to challenging them.
- **The ladder problem:** the entry-level rungs where people learned are disappearing. People who already have hands-on experience gain a growing advantage over new entrants.

## 3. What survives: the common traits
Sources converge on four traits:
1. **Judgment under ambiguity.** Deciding what matters, not producing the output.
2. **Accountability.** A named person owns the outcome (regulated, legal, fiduciary).
3. **Domain expertise plus AI fluency.** Deep subject knowledge used with AI as leverage.
4. **Trust and relationships.** Clients, founders, negotiation.

Roles that mainly *process information* (reports, analysis, communications) face pressure at every level, including leadership.

## 4. Implications for my strategy
- **Get past the entry-level squeeze while I still have the experience edge.** I have ~3 years of hands-on work, and new graduates now struggle to get it. That edge shrinks every year I stay in junior-looking roles.
- **Choose a specialism with all four survival traits.** Investing has judgment, accountability, relationships and domain depth. Product ownership inside the business has judgment, accountability and domain depth. Pure analysis has none.
- **Sell judgment and outcomes, not output.** Evidence logs should show decisions made and results owned ([[core-rules]] impact test), not tools built.
- **Leave information-processing work fast.** That includes VBA tools, reporting and memo review on my CV.
- **Finance is my domain advantage.** Relationship and judgment work there stays human-led longest.

## 5. Best practices for thinking about this
- Separate **exposure** (tasks AI can do) from **displacement** (jobs actually cut). Displacement lags, then arrives as hiring freezes.
- Watch **junior hiring** as the leading indicator, not layoffs.
- Prefer primary sources (Stanford AI Index, Indeed Hiring Lab, Revelio, WEF, government data) over listicles.
- Re-check this note every quarter (see [[founder-mindset]], vision signals).
