# AI Vision

Links: [[index]], [[core-rules]], [[career-direction]], [[founder-mindset]].

*These are premises, not hypotheses. Strategy builds on them; it does not debate them.*

## Premises
- As general technical skills become ubiquitous, only **specialists** and **business owners** remain. Engineers and business analysts disappear, or their skills fold into the skill stack of non-tech people.
- Business analysts and forward deployed engineers cease to exist within 5 years (by ~2031).
- Engineering as a whole transitions into product management, and product management becomes part of the business rather than a standalone function.
- Everyone must now move toward becoming part of the business by becoming a specialist. Those who stay on the engineering path are wiped out; only the top 0.01% remain.

## What survives at full maturity (agreed 2026-10-24)
Work that remains is defined by what AI can't hold. Detail and evidence: [[ai-maturity-jobs]].
1. Accountability holders (named human carries liability).
2. Owners of capital and businesses.
3. Trust and relationship roles.
4. Judgment without objective outcomes.
5. Physical, unstructured work.
6. Holders of scarce context (networks, proprietary data and access).
7. Work not worth automating (low pay).

**Position is the moat, not skills.** Target a stack of 1 + 2 + 3 + 6. Skill-only roles are fragile. 5 and 7 are not targets.

## What this means for me
- My current roles (FDE, ex-BA) are bridges, not destinations. Use them to get embedded in a business domain, then exit before 2031.
- Product management and product vision are critical skills: see [[skill-product-management]].
- Technical skills ([[skill-ai-llm-engineering]], [[skill-data-engineering-cloud]]) are tools a specialist uses, not the identity to sell.
