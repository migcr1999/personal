# Specialism Decision (Oct 2026)

Links: [[index]], [[coaching-approach]], [[career-direction]], [[future-of-work]], [[ai-vision]], [[core-rules]], [[decision-log]].

## 1. Reframe
- **Asked:** which specialism should I commit to?
- **Real question:** which path gets me out of information-processing work and into an accountable business-side role fastest, given that HSBC is cutting and my pay is below market?
- **What a pro would add:** (a) HSBC itself is now a risk, not just a slow payer; (b) I need market leverage (BATNA) before the raise talk; (c) check real career trajectories before committing.

**Reads:**
- My pay sits at the bottom of the HK PM band, even though I already do product-adjacent work.
- Junior VC pay is about the same as mine now, so the investing path pays off only at senior levels (partner, network-gated).
- Not yet gathered (only the user can get it): trajectory sample, recruiter pay quotes, practitioner interviews.

## 3. Method: weighted decision matrix (scores 1-5)
| Criterion (weight) | A. AI/tech venture investing | B. AI product owner in a finance business line | C. Vertical AI founder (finance) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survival traits, [[future-of-work]] (30%) | 5 | 4 | 5 |
| Pay and upside (25%) | 3: junior pay ≈ current; top end network-gated | 4: HK$35-65k PM, ~HK$90k+ VP | 3: high variance |
| Hard blockers, [[core-rules]] (15%) | 2: closed-door partner networks; capital to raise a fund | 5: none | 3: limited personal assets |
| Time to credibility (15%) | 2: 3-5 yrs (deal record, Mandarin) | 4: 6-18 months (FDE + product hybrid now) | 2: needs paying customers |
| Option value (15%) | 4 | 4: leads to C or A | 4 |
| **Weighted score** | **3.45** | **4.15** | **3.75** |

**Scenario check:**
- **Fast AI (PM folds into the business by ~2029):** B holds only if the role sits *inside a business line* (e.g. wealth, payments), not in central tech. C gains.
- **Slow AI:** B is the clear winner; A becomes more reachable over time.

## 4. Recommendation (confidence: medium)
**Commit to B: AI product owner inside a finance business line, as a stepping stone to C (vertical AI founder in finance) around 2028-29.** Keep A parked, not closed: a strong product record in finance AI is itself an entry route into investing later.

- **Why:** highest score, no hard blockers, fastest to credibility, and the FDE + product hybrid gives the bridge now. It also builds the domain and customer-problem knowledge C needs.
- **What would change it:** the trajectory sample shows few people moved into product from my background; recruiters say I'm not credible for PM; or a real deal-team opening appears (re-score A).

## 5. Pre-mortem: "It's 2028 and B failed. Why?"
1. **Product title without real ownership** (still delivering, not deciding). Fix: only accept roles that own a metric and a roadmap.
2. **Caught in HSBC cuts** (non-client-facing middle office). Fix: build an external BATNA now; move toward client-facing or business-line roles.
3. **Sat in central tech product**, which gets absorbed per [[ai-vision]]. Fix: target business-line product roles.

## 7. Challenge round (2026-10-24): competing for a crowded seat
**User's challenge:** AI PM / AI governance seats are the obvious move for every top performer. Usually there is one seat per department, and it goes to someone senior with deep engineering/AI experience. The Ventures MD is anti-engineer.

**Verdict:** the challenge is right. A generic AI PM seat is a losing contest for me. B survives only as a *niche, incumbent* play.

### How to compete: don't enter the contest they're in
1. **Win by incumbency, not application.** Department seats usually go to whoever is already doing the work when the role gets formalised. Do the job before it exists, with measured results, so the role gets written around me.
2. **Go narrower than they can.** A senior AI engineer doesn't know CVC deal flow, portfolio monitoring or investment memos. The intersection "venture investing workflow + hands-on AI" is thin. Own *AI for venture investing*, not AI in general.
3. **Flip the MD's view into my pitch.** The MD wants business people running AI without engineers, which matches [[ai-vision]]. So I am not the FDE serving the investors; I am **the investment professional who already runs AI** and shows the rest of the team how. I am the proof of the MD's thesis, not an exception to it.
4. **Sell business KPIs, not builds.** Deals sourced or screened, investor hours saved, portfolio issues caught early, memo turnaround time. Never "I built an agent".
5. **This reopens A.** An investor-who-runs-AI inside Ventures earns the deal exposure that A needs. B and A converge in the same position, and C draws on the same domain later.
6. **Pick less crowded arenas if internal fails:** CVC/VC firms and fintechs adopting AI, SG or Bangkok, smaller business lines where seniors aren't competing.

### Revised positioning
- **Title to aim for:** Investment Associate (AI) or Ventures AI Lead, with a business title rather than "PM" or "FDE".
- **Story:** "I run the team's AI-enabled sourcing and portfolio work, and make investors self-sufficient with AI."
- **Avoid:** anything framed as engineering support. The MD will discount it, and [[ai-vision]] says it dies.

### New external tests
| Test | Confirms | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Count 20 HK/SG AI PM / AI lead postings and note applicant counts and required seniority | Measures how crowded the seat really is | 2026-11-15 |
| Ask the sponsor how the MD would react to "investor who runs AI" vs "AI PM" framing | MD-compatible scope exists | 2026-11-15 |
| Pick one investment workflow (e.g. deal screening) and run it AI-first for 4 weeks, tracking a business KPI | A measurable result the MD values | 2026-12-15 |

## 8. Correction (2026-10-24): I'm not an investor, and my role is the one the MD wants gone
Section 7's "investor who runs AI" positioning is withdrawn. I have no deal involvement and do sourcing at most. The MD wants the investors to absorb my work, so a dedicated FDE role disappears.

**Verdict:** Ventures is a **time-limited platform**, not a long-term home. There is no AI PM seat to win there.

**Revised play:**
1. **Lead the handover on purpose.** Own the project that makes investors self-sufficient with AI. KPIs: number of investors running workflows unaided, hours saved, sourcing volume. It fits the MD's goal, earns goodwill, and becomes my strongest external evidence.
2. **Make sourcing the domain foothold.** AI-driven deal sourcing (pipeline built, companies screened, deals that reached IC) is the only investor-adjacent outcome I can own. It keeps A possible later.
3. **Land B outside Ventures.** Target an AI product role in a business line (another HSBC business, a fintech or CVC elsewhere) within ~12 months, before the handover finishes.
4. **The external BATNA is now urgent**, not optional.

**Updated tests:**
- Sponsor: is "lead the investor self-sufficiency programme" a scope the MD will back? Due 2026-11-15.
- Sourcing KPIs to date (companies sourced, reached IC). Due 2026-11-08.

## 9. Counterpoints (2026-10-24): sponsor promoted, investing route open
**New facts:** the manager becomes Head of AI Products in Ventures and would take me along if she leaves. A UK Tech grad is learning investing hands-on.

**Verdict:** section 8 stays the primary plan, but the time pressure drops and two routes open up.

**Revised plan:**
1. **Main track (B, internal):** move from FDE to **AI product in her new team.** Products the investors use themselves, which fits the MD's view. Lead the investor self-sufficiency work as the first product.
2. **Second track (A, via SEA):** source a **Southeast Asia deal**. Native Thai plus Bangkok access is an edge most HK colleagues lack. Ask to follow it into diligence, the way the UK grad did. Mandarin becomes a slow side investment, not a gate.
3. **External BATNA:** keep it, but at a lower intensity. Its main job now is pay leverage and insurance against relying on one sponsor.

**Risks:**
- **Relying on one sponsor.** If she leaves or loses influence, my position goes with her. Build a second senior relationship.
- **The MD still sees product as FDE under a new name.** Products must make investors self-sufficient, not need me in the loop.
- **Pay is still flat.** The sponsor's promotion is the moment to ask for a regrade.

**Tests:**
- Ask the sponsor about a formal role and title in her team, plus a raise. Due 2026-11-30.
- Find 3 SEA companies that fit the Ventures thesis and pitch one internally. Due 2026-12-15.
- Talk to the UK grad: how he got onto deals. Due 2026-11-15.

## 10. Correction (2026-10-24): how Ventures deals work, and where the market is
Section 9's Track 2 ("source an SEA deal, Thai edge") is withdrawn. Thailand is weak, and deals need a strategic fit, a POC and an MD sponsor ([[hsbc-ventures-model]], [[asia-venture-landscape]]).

**Revised Track 2: get into deals through POCs.** The POC is where AI product work and investing overlap.
1. Ask to run or shape the AI POCs between startups and HSBC business units. It's product work my sponsor's team can own, and it puts me inside the deal process.
2. Source from business pain points: find what a business unit needs, then the startup that solves it.
3. Build relationships with the MD-level business heads who sponsor deals. They're also my second-sponsor hedge.
4. Mandarin: decide by 2027-01-31 whether to commit. It unlocks China, the main market; Singapore/SEA is the English-only alternative.

**Tests:**
- Map the last 5 Ventures deals: business partner, POC owner, sponsoring MD. Due 2026-11-30.
- Ask the sponsor whether her AI Products team can own POC delivery. Due 2026-11-30.
